From the dual invasion of Poland in 1939 to the fall of Berlin in 1945, these campaigns decided the course of the war on land, at sea, and in the air. Each page combines operational narrative, force and casualty figures drawn from scholarly ranges, maps, and curated sources for further reading.

Sources & Methodology

Echoes of Conflict is an educational synthesis, not a substitute for archival monographs. Battle pages are generated from structured research notes covering strategy, doctrine, technology, combat narrative, human cost, opposing-side perspectives, unusual details, maps, and curated further reading.

Statistics. Wartime force strengths and casualties are often disputed. Definitions differ (killed vs. all casualties; single-day totals vs. whole campaigns; which units are counted). Sidebars therefore use widely cited museum, official-history, and academic ranges where exact figures are unsettled, and they avoid false precision.

Narrative & myths. Core accounts follow mainstream scholarly consensus (for example work associated with the National WWII Museum, Imperial War Museums, US Army Center of Military History, Naval History and Heritage Command, and major campaign historians). Famous myths and neat set-pieces—Polish cavalry “charging tanks,” a planned Pearl Harbor “third wave,” Midway’s pure “five fatal minutes”—are treated carefully, with contested claims flagged on the relevant pages.

Sources on each page. Every battle ends with external links to museums, memorials, and document collections. Prefer those primary and institutional sources when citing or researching further. Quotes appear in narrative context; some well-known lines survive partly through memoir and should be read with that caution.
